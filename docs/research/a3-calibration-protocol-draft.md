# Draft A3 protocol for calibration at a fixed horizon

**Decision sought.** Can a simple recalibration improve the accuracy of eligible market probabilities on later, previously unseen events? Start with one authorized source and mechanism. The strongest current candidate is one Good Judgment Project experimental market arm. Kalshi would be a separate population with separate permission. No A3 execution or acquisition is authorized by this draft. [A2 findings](a2-data-feasibility.md) · [Source evidence](a2-source-register.md)

## Primary question and population

For a quoted YES probability `p` and final binary outcome `Y`, the calibration null is **E[Y | p] = p** in the designated population and horizon. Under the alternative, observed outcome frequencies differ systematically. A separate predictive question is whether a pre-fitted logistic recalibration has lower future expected Brier loss than the raw market baseline. A reliability plot diagnoses calibration, while paired loss on new events tests the value of the fitted correction.

Before inspecting holdout outcomes, name the source, experimental arm or venue, mechanism, eligible categories, dates, quote type, horizon, settlement policy, event weights and decision criteria. Enumerate all opened, ordinary, unconditional binary questions inside that frame, including inactive ones. Require a unit-normalized binary YES payout, unambiguous side mapping and documented rules. Keep cancellations, voids, scalar payouts, variants, disputed results, unresolved events and unavailable observations in the coverage denominator with distinct reasons. Record result vintage and any corrections. Score only final, verified binary outcomes.

Give the real-world question an event group and each distinct contract a stable side-normalized ID. In GJP retain condition, full question ID including branch suffix and answer option. In Kalshi retain series, event and market tickers plus a reviewed crosswalk for related events. Cluster duplicate questions and dependent outcomes from the same contest, release or tournament before splitting. Exclude conditioning branches from the primary study. Keep at most one observation per contract and horizon. Count observed rows, contracts, event groups and resolved/scored groups separately. A single question represented by YES and NO sides contributes once.

## Information available at decision time

1. Propose a primary horizon **24 hours before the scheduled close as known then**, with seven days and one hour as secondary diagnostics. Freeze the scheduling rule and timezone. For reschedules require the historical announcement sequence and a reproducible decision time. Exclude a case whose schedule, outcome timing or availability cannot be reconstructed. Exclude a primary cutoff before opening, after suspension, or after the event outcome was knowable, even if settlement came later.
2. Lock one price definition per study. For the GJP market-scoring-rule arm, propose the most recent verifiable post-fill YES market probability available at cutoff from an ordinary transaction. Verify percent units, batch initialization, ordering, time zone and when post-fill prices were visible. The participant's belief field is a separate variable. For an authorized Kalshi study, propose the latest non-block YES trade price normalized to a binary unit payout. Predefine a maximum age (candidate **24 hours** for the primary horizon), record actual age, and test the effect of staleness on coverage in development data.
3. Both the source timestamp and the time the observation could have become known must be at or before cutoff. Record timestamp precision, time zone, publication lag evidence and any conservative delay applied. With unresolved ordering inside a time bucket, select a uniquely verified last price or mark missing. In particular, two different prices in a minute marked 12:00 cannot be ordered by an arbitrary ID. Never use future interpolation, final-market snapshots or settlement as substitutes. If evidence supports only date precision, design a separate day-level protocol or stop.
4. A two-sided contemporaneous midpoint or a completed one-minute trade close could be a **separately defined sensitivity analysis**. Specify quote staleness, synchronization, spread and complete interval rules in advance. Exclude synthetic bars and null sides. Do not fill a missing primary observation from another definition. Record missingness by horizon, category, activity, time period and event. Present complete-case coverage alongside score estimates. An inferred effect applies only to that observed population unless selection can be addressed.

## Chronological design and scoring

- **Splits.** Propose contiguous development, validation and final holdout eras, approximately 60/20/20 by distinct groups, with actual calendar boundaries chosen from counts and dates before viewing evaluation labels. Reserve A2-inspected outcomes and their related groups for development, or exclude them if outside that era. Keep related contracts, horizons and experimental duplicates in the same split. Purge groups spanning a boundary. At each model-fit date use only outcomes already finalized and available then. Apply a time gap if overlapping forecast windows or release lags require one. Use forward-only validation inside development if tuning is needed. If the archive cannot support three meaningful eras, report descriptive results and defer the predictive claim.
- **Forecasts.** Baseline is the recorded market probability. Candidate is logistic recalibration with intercept and slope, `q = sigmoid(a + b × logit(p))`. The identity parameters are a = 0 and b = 1. Use a predeclared epsilon (proposed 0.0001) for the input logit and for both models' log-loss calculations. Report endpoint frequency. Retain raw baseline probabilities for Brier scoring. Estimate parameters with event-weighted logistic loss in development. Any regularization and final refit rule must be frozen on validation before the holdout is opened. If data are degenerate or the fit fails, report the failure.
- **Estimand.** Compute paired `Brier = (p − Y)²` and binary log loss `−[Y ln(p) + (1−Y) ln(1−p)]` for both forecasts on identical cases. Average contracts within event group, then weight groups equally. Primary contrast is candidate minus baseline mean Brier, so negative favors recalibration. Show both absolute scores, the paired difference and uncertainty. Report contract-weighted scores secondarily for comparison with A1's contract-level evaluator. Keep log loss secondary.
- **Diagnostics.** Plot mean predicted probability against observed YES frequency in fixed, predeclared bins on [0,1] with the identity line. Give the contract and group counts, event-weighted frequencies and uncertainty per bin. Show sparse and empty bins, tails, changes in market composition, and prospective versus retrospective sample coverage. Candidate favorite–longshot patterns, category effects and horizon differences are exploratory unless preregistered as separate tests.
- **Uncertainty and sample adequacy.** Preserve whole event groups when resampling paired predictions. If releases or tournaments induce temporal dependence, resample time blocks containing complete related groups, or limit the inference when too few blocks exist. Use a paired 95% interval and report sensitivity to the grouping choice. Before holdout, specify the smallest Brier improvement worth distinguishing and the desired interval width. Estimate the variance of paired event losses, tail-bin support, dependence and missingness from development data, and use blocked resampling to project precision. A large trade count cannot compensate for few independent outcomes. An imprecise interval calls for an inconclusive result.
- **Repeated testing.** Freeze the population, primary horizon, primary paired Brier comparison, model, exclusions, bins and reporting template. Allocate multiplicity control (e.g. Holm adjustment) to any predeclared family of secondary confirmatory tests. Label all other probes exploratory. One holdout read and a retained analysis manifest. Any alteration motivated by holdout findings requires a new evaluation cohort.

## Gates and prospective validation

| Gate | Evidence required to proceed |
| --- | --- |
| Scope and rights | Operator approval for the named source, exact fields, byte/time cap, local use, fitting, sharing, retention and cost. Verify the license/version again. Kalshi requires written project-specific permission. |
| Point-in-time records | Documented identifier/branch mapping and price units on a bounded sample, verified settlement states, schedule and timezone, publication and tie-order evidence. Resolve or quantify duplicates, voids and missing periods. |
| Scientific adequacy | Coverage by period, distinct event counts after exclusions, a defensible split, predeclared effect size and attainable precision for the intended claim. Restrict to a descriptive study if these fail. |
| Frozen evaluation | Register one baseline, candidate, comparison and inference procedure with independent review before releasing holdout labels. Retain only permitted provenance and outputs. |
| Prospective evidence | For a new authorized population, timestamp and freeze predictions before outcomes, log all eligible opportunities and missing cases, and score after final resolution. Fix an end date or precision rule in advance. A closed GJP archive permits historical holdout only. A different live source requires its own approval and a new population-specific study. |

An out-of-time Brier improvement would be evidence about forecast accuracy in the tested population. A claim about net returns would additionally require permitted executable bid/ask and size histories, fees, latency, fill and exposure assumptions, and independent prospective evidence after costs. No return estimate or order policy follows from this protocol.
